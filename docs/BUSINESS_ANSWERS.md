## What is Caprae's Mission?

Caprae Capital's mission is to become the operating infrastructure for
entrepreneurship through acquisition (ETA) — not just a source of capital, but
the partner that makes a searcher's entire journey from sourcing to
post-acquisition value creation faster, more disciplined, and less lonely.
That shows up concretely: the firm works with dozens of active searchers, has
built its own AI-powered sourcing tool (SaaSquatch) rather than renting one,
and has recently expanded into sell-side work and partnerships with advisory
firms like Third Equity Partners and Mirante Partners to support first-time
buyers through the hardest parts of a deal.

What sets this apart from a typical PE mandate is the time horizon. Caprae
treats an acquisition as the *start* of a seven-year value-creation process,
not the finish line of a transaction. That reframes what "returns" means: less
about the multiple paid at close, more about whether the business is
meaningfully better — more efficient, better positioned, technologically
current — by the time it's handed off or exited. AI adoption is one of the
main levers in that thesis, not a side initiative: a lot of the SMB market
Caprae targets is exactly the profile of a good business that never had the
resources or urgency to modernize, and closing that gap is where the real
value gets created.

The SaaSquatch product is the clearest evidence of this mission in practice.
Caprae didn't buy a sourcing tool — they built one, iterated on it in public,
and are now using this very challenge to find people who can keep extending
it. That's a mission stated as a product decision, not just a slide.

## How is Caprae Changing the ETA Space and Broader PE?

Most of the ETA ecosystem still runs on manually built spreadsheets, cold
outreach with no scoring, and advisors who are paid for hours rather than
outcomes. Caprae is attacking that from two directions at once: building
proprietary AI tooling (SaaSquatch) instead of licensing a generic CRM or
data vendor — a tool whose own site already reports serving "2,000+ sales
teams" and "30+ Entrepreneurs and Searchers" without differentiating the
criteria between them, which is precisely the gap this submission targets —
and building an advisory layer — partnerships with firms guiding
first-time buyers through diligence, LOIs, and financing — so a searcher isn't
navigating both sourcing and deal mechanics alone. The "Search as a Service"
model and initiatives like the ETA Hub cohort extend that further: instead of
selling searchers a tool and wishing them luck, Caprae is building the
surrounding infrastructure — buy-box definition, verified prospect lists,
live outreach practice — that makes the search itself more likely to succeed.

Against broader PE, the differentiator is the seven-year, post-acquisition
lens. Most PE returns are driven by financial engineering and multiple
expansion at exit; Caprae's stated thesis is that the greater value creation
happens *after* the deal closes, through operational and technological
improvement. That's a genuinely different risk model — it requires being
right about a business's *improvability*, not just its current numbers — and
it's why a scoring signal like "under-digitized but otherwise strong" (the
core idea behind the LeadRank submission) is directly useful to this specific
strategy in a way it wouldn't be to a firm optimizing for leverage and exit
timing alone.
